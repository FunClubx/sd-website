Privacy Policy for All Format Video Player - Mixx

This policy applies to All Format Video Player - Mixx, also called Mixx Player (Android package: com.mixplayer.video.music). The developer is listed on Google Play as Live Wallpaper Studio. The company responsible for the app is Guangzhou Suide Trading Co., Ltd. (广州穗德贸易有限公司), referred to in this policy as “we” or “us”.

Last updated: 8 November 2026 · Privacy contact: microtechdevteam@gmail.com

You do not need a Mixx Player account to play local media. The player reads files on your device to build your library. Other parts of the app use network services, including for ads, usage measurement, crash reporting and features you choose to use.

Your library and permissions

When you grant access, the app scans accessible files to list and play your videos and music. It reads information such as file names and locations, titles, artists, albums, artwork, thumbnails, duration and format. It keeps a local library, playlists, recent items, playback positions and settings so you can find media and resume playback. Building the local library does not require uploading your media files to us.

On older versions of Android this uses storage permissions. On newer versions it uses video or audio permissions; some devices let you grant access to selected videos only. You can also open a file that Android makes available to the player. Actions such as saving subtitles, moving or deleting files, or setting a ringtone may require additional access or confirmation. Notification or overlay access, where used, supports features such as playback controls and the pop-up player.

Private Videos. You set a PIN on your device to control access to this section. The app handles the PIN, private media, thumbnails and information needed to find or restore files locally. The PIN is not a server login, and the feature is not an online backup. If you want to keep these videos, restore them to a normal folder and make a separate backup before clearing app data or uninstalling. Removing the app or its data may make private files inaccessible.

When you use a network feature

  • Network folders and streams. Browsing can discover devices on your local network. When you open a share or stream, the app connects to its server. The server can receive your IP address, the requested path or URL and any credentials needed for that connection.
  • Casting and sharing. When you cast media, the chosen receiver needs the media stream or its address and playback information. When you share a file through Android, the app or person you choose receives the file or access to it.
  • Subtitles and online lookups. A subtitle search can send OpenSubtitles the requested language and search terms, or matching information such as a file name, file size and file hash. Online artwork or metadata requests, where used, send the lookup information to the service providing the result. These requests expose your IP address to the destination.

Usage measurement, diagnostics and ads

Google Analytics for Firebase. We measure how people use the app and where features fail. Events can include app opens and returns, permission choices, media counts, playback starts, duration and errors, playlist and private-folder actions, rating-prompt responses, and ad impressions or clicks. Reports can associate events with an app instance and include device model, OS and app version, language, timestamps and approximate region. For example, we compare first ad exposure with later returns or measured app removals to understand whether ads disrupt use. These measurements concern actions and outcomes; tracking a private-folder action does not require sending the private video or PIN.

Firebase Crashlytics and Remote Config. Crashlytics helps diagnose crashes and freezes using installation identifiers, device and app details, stack traces and diagnostic context such as the last screen or event. Remote Config uses installation and app information to deliver settings such as feature availability and ad or prompt rules. See Firebase privacy and security information.

Advertising. We use Unity LevelPlay with ironSource Ads and Unity Ads to request, deliver and measure ads. Depending on your device, region and settings, these services may process the Android advertising ID when available, other device or app-instance identifiers, IP address, approximate location inferred from IP, device and connection information, and ad requests, impressions and interactions. These data support ad selection (including personalization where applicable), measurement, frequency management and fraud prevention. We also record ad network, placement and impression revenue to evaluate ad performance. See Unity’s app user privacy policy for its partners and privacy choices.

Our own promotions. Some entries promote our other apps. Loading promotional artwork or videos makes a request to our hosting service, which receives connection information such as your IP address. We measure displays and interactions. If you follow a promotion, the store link may include a campaign referral tag.

Feedback and support

If you submit in-app feedback, our feedback service receives the reasons you select, any text you enter, and technical details to help investigate the issue: manufacturer and model, Android version and build, display density, locale country, package name and app version. If you send feedback by email, we also receive your sender address and the contents of the email you send. You do not need to include a media file, PIN or password in your report.

A separate diagnostic email option prepares a message addressed to the VLC Android crash-report mailbox (vlc.crashreport+androidcrash@gmail.com). If you send it, that recipient receives your sender address, app logs and crash reports. If you choose to include the media-library database, the attachment may contain file names, paths and library metadata. Review the recipient, message and attachments before sending. This is separate from automatic Crashlytics reporting.

Sharing, safeguards and retention

Google/Firebase, Unity and its advertising partners, and our hosting and support service providers receive the categories of information described above for their respective purposes. Other recipients depend on the actions you take, such as selecting a cast receiver, file server or email provider. Providers may process information outside your country. We may also disclose information where legally required.

The app relies on Android’s permission controls to limit access to local media. Feedback submissions use HTTPS by default. For a stream or network share, the protection of the connection depends on the protocol and server you choose. A local PIN restricts access through the app; it does not replace device security.

Library records and preferences can persist until you remove them or clear app storage. Files in shared storage may remain after uninstalling. Android backup or device-transfer settings may affect which app data are restored. Analytics, diagnostics and ad records have separate retention periods determined by the service, its configuration and any reporting exports; clearing the app does not delete records already sent. We retain support correspondence as needed to investigate and follow up on reports and meet applicable legal obligations. Contact us about deletion of information you submitted.

Your choices and privacy requests

  • Media access: In Android Settings, open Apps → Mixx Player → Permissions to change or revoke access. Available options vary by Android version. Revoking access limits future reads; clearing app storage removes local app records and settings. Restore private videos first if you want to keep them.
  • Advertising: Use your device’s Ads or Privacy settings to reset or delete the advertising ID or limit personalization where available. These controls do not remove all ads. Unity also describes provider-specific choices in its policy above.
  • Access or deletion: Email microtechdevteam@gmail.com with the subject “Mixx Player privacy request”. Describe the information or report involved and, for email correspondence, the address you used. Do not send a PIN or private video. We may need limited information to verify the request. Some records are linked only to an installation or have been aggregated and cannot be located by name or email. Rights and exceptions vary by jurisdiction.

If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us at the same address so we can investigate. We will post changes to this policy on this page and update the date above.
